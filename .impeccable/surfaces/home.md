- Audience: Bangladeshi public + diaspora + researchers/journalists, skeptical, checking whether the museum's history is "real/clean" before trusting or sharing it.
- Job: read history, cross-check controversies against verification reports, cite sourced facts, watch/open linked interview videos.
- Action: navigate clause-by-clause; no conversion action, no CTA funnel.
- Proof: every claim traceable to a source; Controversies and Verification Reports sections must read as linked, not contradictory in isolation.

- User explicitly chose visual richness over thematic literalism this round: picked Nakshi Kantha (a craft metaphor) over continuing to push the more literal but visually restrained Shaheed Minar monument direction, after direct comparison.

## Direction contract

**THESIS:** The archive is stitched, not printed, carved, or typeset — a Nakshi Kantha quilt where every motif and cross-reference is a visible running-stitch thread, refusing the quiet stone register of the retired monument direction for genuine, saturated, hand-worked richness.

**OWN-WORLD:** Nakshi Kantha embroidery system. Unbleached cotton/muslin cloth ground with visible weave texture. Dense multicolor running-stitch borders and motifs (florals, paisleys, geometric kalka patterns) framing every content block. A full saturated palette used at page scale, not as a thin accent — indigo, deep red, mustard yellow, forest green, magenta — each carrying a named structural role (section wayfinding colors rotate through the palette). Bold rounded Bengali display lettering evoking hand-embroidered lettering for the nameplate. Citations carry a stitched name-tag convention. Cross-references between clauses render as a literal stitched thread line (dashed running-stitch path) connecting related items, not a plain text link — kantha embroidery already links motifs across cloth this way. Verification states render as stitch density/pattern, never color: satin-dense solid = verified, cross-hatch = disputed, loose running-stitch (widely spaced dashes) = referred/pending. A circular running-stitch emblem patch (appliqué center, stitched outline ring) replaces the monument silhouette as the site's mark.

**STORY:** The visitor arrives to a warm, richly patterned, unmistakably Bengali handmade surface — color and pattern signal care and cultural rootedness. They read clauses as stitched panels, follow literal thread-lines to cross-referenced verification, and trust the record because every claim is visibly "sewn" to its source and its rebuttal, not just asserted in prose.

**FIRST VIEWPORT:** Full-bleed unbleached cloth-textured hero. A large embroidered-style nameplate in bold rounded lettering, deep indigo thread-color, inside a dense multicolor running-stitch border frame. The circular stitched emblem patch inset at one corner of the masthead. Below it, the six sections render as stitched "panels" in a patchwork-style grid — each panel bordered in a different rotating accent thread color for wayfinding — not a plain numbered list. Genuinely colorful first viewport: multiple saturated hues visible at once.

**FORM:** Nakshi Kantha embroidery system. Own grounded candidate, index 3 of 7 in the original playful/rich-themed roll (rickshaw/truck art, patachitra scroll painting, **nakshi kantha embroidery [chosen, this round, by direct user comparison against Shaheed Minar]**, Eid/wedding ornamental print, comic/graphic-novel panels, carnival poster, children's picture-book spread). Seed key `2434023b`, mode `read`. Raised with: (1) stitched-thread cross-reference discipline (declined challenger `games-toys-physics-play-brick-build-instructions`, donated its wordless-arrow teaching clarity as a literal stitched thread line); (2) stitch-density-as-state discipline (declined challenger `operate-a-cutting-bench-select-rail`, donated its "state is a mark not a hue" principle, applied as stitch pattern density).

**FINISH:** unreviewed and undocumented is unfinished; this build ends with the finish review, the verdict, DESIGN.md, and every shipping raster carrying its provenance.
